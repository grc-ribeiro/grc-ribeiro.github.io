GRC 3060: Assignments

Final project: dashboard draft

For this lab, you will build a complete working draft of your final project dashboard in Tableau Public. Your dashboard should answer one central question using at least five related visualizations.

Your dashboard does not need to be fully polished by the end of the lab. However, it should be complete enough for a viewer to understand, explore, and test.

Define your dashboard’s purpose

Before building your dashboard, identify the central question it will answer.

Your question should be specific enough to give your dashboard a clear focus, but broad enough to be explored through multiple visualizations.

Examples

How have enrollment patterns changed across colleges over time?

A dashboard answering this question might include visualizations showing overall enrollment, enrollment by college, changes over time, and comparisons between groups.

Other examples might include

  • How do housing costs compare with household incomes across cities?

  • How do wildfire frequency and severity vary across California?

  • How do flight delays vary by airport, airline, and season?

  • How have team performance and scoring changed across seasons?

  • How have movie box office earnings changed across genres over time?

  • How do rates of chronic diseases vary across states and age groups?

Write down

  1. Your central question.

  2. The audience for your dashboard.

  3. The main finding or takeaway you want your audience to understand.

  4. How each visualization will contribute to answering your question.

Your main finding may change as you explore your data. You can revise it as you work.

Build your visualizations

Create or revise the worksheets you will use in your dashboard.

Your dashboard must include at least five related visualizations. Each visualization should contribute a different part of the answer to your central question.

As you work, consider

  • Is each chart appropriate for the data and the question it addresses?

  • Does each chart add useful information?

  • Are titles and labels clear?

  • Are colors used consistently across the visualizations?
    For example, if a college is represented by a particular color in one chart, use the same color for that college in the other charts whenever possible.

  • Are the text and marks large enough to read?

  • Are tooltips understandable and useful?

Assemble your dashboard

Create a new dashboard in Tableau Public and add your visualizations.

Arrange the dashboard so viewers can understand its purpose and know where to look first. Consider which visualization or finding is most important, and use size, position, headings, and whitespace to give it emphasis.

Your dashboard must include

  • A clear dashboard title.

  • At least five related visualizations.

  • Chart titles or labels where necessary.

  • A logical layout and visual hierarchy.

  • Consistent colors and formatting.

  • A source note identifying where the data came from.

  • A short caption, annotation, or text box explaining the main finding.

The title should communicate what the dashboard is about. The main finding should tell viewers something the data shows, rather than simply repeat the title.

For example:

Enrollment by College, 2015–2025
Enrollment increased overall, but the rate of growth varied considerably across colleges.

Add interactivity

Add at least one interactive feature that helps viewers answer a question or explore the data.

You may use:

  • A filter.

  • A parameter.

  • A detailed tooltip.

Choose an interaction that serves a purpose. For example, a filter could let viewers compare different years, or selecting a category in one chart could update another chart.

Make sure viewers can understand the dashboard before interacting with it. If an interaction is not obvious, add a short instruction, such as:

“Select a college to see its enrollment trend.”

Test the feature to make sure it works as intended.
