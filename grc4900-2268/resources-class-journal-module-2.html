Class journal, module 2

One of my concerns was how we want to be able to ensure that no theft will occur with items in the cart.

That’s a valid concern, but we are not focusing on this problem, because that’s a organization problem, not a user one. We want to focus on the users’ experience. You may want to mention this concern under “Possible constraints” in your report, but we don’t need to explore or expand too much on the issue.

Something interesting I learned in my user experience writing course is that friction isn’t always something we should eliminate for users. In some situations, intentional friction can encourage users to slow down and think before making an important decision, and maybe I can translate this to the project we are working on.

Correct. As long as it doesn’t become an annoyance, intentional friction can be beneficial to the user experience.

Assumptions about demographics are not very relevant for knowing the audience for your product.

Correct. They are not.

Comparing your product to those currently existing in the market can help highlight areas of need. Understanding where a substitute is lacking can help you understand areas that need improvement.

Exactly.

I used variable in InDesign for the first time which was a new fun trick!

Yes. There are some InDesign tricks sneaking into this class.

You can include images in the flow to adjust with the layout.

And for this report, you have to.

A system for collaboration that worked for us was working together on a Google Doc, having a OneDrive folder for images, and trading off who exports the InDesign document each week.

Solid plan.

You do not need to have an entirely original visual language! A lot of the time, the colors, symbols, and typography used in certain products are very meaningful. Taking inspiration from similar products is not always a bad thing.

“Don’t try to be original; just try to be good.” (Paul Rand)

I liked the reminder in lecture of not needing to replace what already works. There’s no need to reinvent the wheel from scratch, and designers can improve pain points that exist.

“Innovate as your last resource.” (Charles Eames).

An idea can be good but you have to figure out how your product resonates with the audience. What features would be useful and how to output those ideas.

That’s our next step.
