GRC 4900: Resources

Class journal, module 9

I love overthinking a lot but this was one of the first projects where I’ve been told to overthink, and I feel it really helped my color palette and fonts shine through since I put so much intention into them.

Sometimes you really have to take your time and think for longer than most would find sensible. It happens when you care about your work. In any field.

One of my favorite parts of designing is designing with intentionality.

You’re my people.

It was a good reminder to remember that useful and usable are different and not interchangeable.

I figured some of you could use that reminder.

This is my first time really getting detailed in Figma and it’s been a learning curve figuring out how to do what I want to do. The videos have been helpful and so is Google.

I’m glad the videos are helpful. And assisting with software is one point where Gemini has been quite helpful for me.

Every design decision has an effect on the user so it is important to make them consciously.

Exactly.

I found myself creating variations too similar to one another and will try to create more contrasting versions.

It happens.

I’m curious to see how many steps we add when we are incorporating the graphics because I’ve already seen some places that need to be added.

Design is never done.

Defining the brand personality and tone is a good starting point when working out the visual language.

You have to. Although, you may find out that you disagree with your teammates.

I don’t love much of what I’ve designed so far, so I need to look for more designs that I do love that I can use as inspiration, and iterate from there.

Use your references consciously.

It was cool to see different interpretations of the same words for brand personality and tone.

Thanks! It was fun coming up with them too. I remember that I asked ChatGPT for some ideas, and they were not very helpful.

The tip to use another example as a starting point was very useful. I used Formi as inspiration for my first layout.

That’s a good strategy.

I want to investigate how user testing can reveal design issues that may not be noticeable when designers evaluate their own work.

That’s a fascinating subfield of design. It’s not that easy to plan, to conduct, or to analyze good usability tests.

It’s interesting how the aesthetic usability effect is so unconscious in how we interact with different interfaces daily, but now I realize how apparent it is when I perform daily tasks.

The effect is much stronger than most realize.

I like using coolors.co to help me find a jumping off point for color palette.

That’s a great resource.

When working on my layouts, I had to keep in mind to not compromise accessibility for aesthetics. Figma’s built-in color contrast checker was helpful for this.

Very good point. And very good tip about Figma’s built-in color contrast checker.

This week’s color and background color prompt is fun and easy.

Let’s see them then!
