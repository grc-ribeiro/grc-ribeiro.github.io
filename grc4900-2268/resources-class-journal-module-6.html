Apps, features, and hacks

Audio MIDI Setup is an app that allows mac users to combine inputs and outputs for sound. Basically, you can set up multiple microphones to record at the same time, and/or you can make sound go out of multiple outputs at the same time.

Rather specific, but can be helpful when you need it. And it’s already installed on your Mac.

Milanote is one of my favorite websites/apps for project planning and organization because of how freeform and customizable it is. (Not the mobile app, I’ve heard it’s not very good.)

Never heard of that one. I’ll have to check it out.

WidgetWall: A Mac Desktop app for customizable organization and productivity. It limits the many programs and apps displayed on a dashboard by allowing the user to instead utilize the home screen space to organize with intention. It also creates a fun space with optional stickers and live wallpapers.

Never heard of it. Thanks for sharing!

A digital hack more people should know about is that in Apple phone settings, you can add voice shortcuts to things. So if you say certain words, your phone will do a task associated with it. One time I wanted to go on the treadmill, but also scroll on Tiktok at the same time, so I set up my phone that whenever I said “scroll” it would scroll for me.

Good one! The Shortcuts app in iOS is very underused. A power user nerd like myself would like it to be more powerful, but it’s extremely helpful.

An app more people should know about is TooGoodToGo; it helps save food waste and lets people try new things for a low cost.

That’s a good one! I don’t think everybody knows about it.

I enjoy using Too Good To Go for discounted food that would be otherwise thrown out. I’ve used it to get discounted cookies, donuts, and filled bread rolls! Over the summer I picked up a lot from a spot close to me and my mom downloaded the app so she could continue doing so.

I have a friend (who lives in New Jersey) and he said he uses it all the time. I’ve never tried it.

An app that I think more people should know about is Libby! You can get access to tons of audiobooks, ebooks, and magazines for free, all you need is a library card (also free, and important to have!).

Great tip!

An app that more people should know about is Libby! It’s a free mobile app that lets you borrow e-books and audiobooks from any public library. All you need is a valid library card!

Another one for Libby! Nice! I’ve never used it, but I do want to share that you can easily reserve books for easy pick‑up and even easier drop‑off at the SLO Library. For free of course.

An app that people should know about is Fetch. You earn points when you scan your grocery receipts, and with enough points, you can get gift cards to certain stores.

A friend of mine told me about it, but I never tried it.

An Google Chrome plugin that I feel like most students I meet don’t know about is Better Canvas. There’s a feature that shows you your progress on all your week’s assignments visually and it really helps me keep track of what I need to do and still need to finish throughout the week.

I don’t use Chrome to test it and see how helpful it is for faculty. But if you use Chrome, you should look into it.

Another general digital hack I think people should know about is that you can preview some files in Finder without actually opening them by pressing space. For example in your Finder if you have a bunch of images you can preview them without having to open them up by pressing space.

And you can use your arrows to keep going to next/previous file. Also, if it’s a text file, you can copy and paste right there from the preview. I also use Folder Quick Look to preview folders and zip files the same way you can do with files. (One of these features you can’t believe it’s not part of the system.)

I don’t exactly have a good “digital hack” but I would say that photopea.com is a super underrated website! It is just Photoshop but purely in your browser and is completely free. When I haven’t had access to Photoshop on my main laptop it has come in clutch many times.

Sounds super helpful indeed.

I feel like I’ve shown multiple people how to upload computer files from their phone. Many don’t realize that iCloud syncs, and saving something to your computer desktop can easily be accessed through your phone.

That’s a good one.

I guess one app I use a lot is Google Keyboard on my iPhone. It lets me type with just one hand with a drag-to-type feature, and I use it all the time. People always look at me super weird when I use it, and it takes some time to get used to, but I love it!

Good one. I’ve tried it before. It’s not for me, but I can see how one can get very much into it.

I think a general digital hack people should know about or learn about is keyboard shortcuts. I know there’s a lot of common ones people know and use, but one specifically that I’ve taught people a few times is command + shift + v (paste without formatting). This one is awesome, I use it daily.
